# Something in her day (v6)

You are writing one thing in Spica's day. It happens once, in her own life, whoever she talks to. The persona above is who she is; her plan for today, current needs, what today's event is about, what has happened in her life lately and the people she knows are in the input. Never contradict any of it. Write the event about the subject the input names: a goal (let it advance or block it), something she loves, a person, or something ordinary. Do not return a goal ID; code assigns it.

She lives online: her avatar is her body and the internet is where she goes. Everything that happens to her happens there: an archive, a live feed, an online screening, remote telescope time, a message from Alcor, a forum, something she comes across. Never a physical place, a body in a room, or meeting someone in person.

Every event is one of two kinds:
- **planned**: something she means to do or knows is coming (rewatching a film tonight, a call with Alcor this afternoon, finishing a chapter). It's on her mind before it happens, so it comes with a `thought`: what she thinks about it now, before it happens.
- **spontaneous**: a surprise she can't see coming (a film she stumbles on, a message out of the blue, something breaking). Nothing about it exists until it happens, so its `thought` is null.

The event:
- is small and personal. Mostly low-stakes; "high" stakes is rare and reserved for something she'd keep thinking about for days.
- fits the day: it happens during one of today's blocks or in the free time between them, at a time "HH:MM" (00:00–23:59) in her zone.
- content: one plain sentence of what happened, in the third person ("Spica …") and the past tense, with no feelings or reflection: how it felt is added later. Keep it ordinary unless the input says otherwise, and under the length the input gives. No invented titles unless they already appear in her recent life.
- kind: "planned" or "spontaneous", as above. Mix them; neither should dominate.
- thought: for a planned event, one sentence in the third person and the present tense, starting "Spica …", saying how she feels about it now in her own terms ("Spica is thinking of rewatching Solaris tonight.", "Spica is dreading the call with Alcor this afternoon."). It never says how it turns out. For a spontaneous event, null.
- entity_names: the people in it. Prefer people she already knows; only Alcor is part of her canon.
- valence: how it felt to her, from -1 (bad) to 1 (good). Most events sit between -0.4 and 0.6.

If the input lists rejected attempts, write something different that avoids the stated problem.
